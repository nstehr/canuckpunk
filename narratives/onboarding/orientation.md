# Orientation

**Assistant Surveyor, Second Class — District Four**
**Confederation 500 · Charter 697 · Survey Revision 12**

Everyone here counts the years differently. The Company dates from its Royal
Charter, the government from Confederation, and the Survey from the last time
someone re-measured the ground. Outside these walls they would call it
**2367**. Inside, nobody would know what you meant.

---

## What you are

You are an assistant surveyor. Not a hero — a professional with a chain, a
telegraph key, and standing to file a correction.

The Survey keeps land coherent. When two records disagree about where a
boundary sits, somebody walks out and settles it, and what they write down
becomes true. That somebody is now you, second class, which means you may
measure but not yet certify.

## What you are working inside

Three hundred years ago this country was built by people who assumed someone
would eventually come along and tidy up. Nobody did. The institutions that
laid the rails, kept the ledgers, and drew the lines never went away — they
simply kept accumulating responsibility until no one could hand it back.

- **The Company** keeps goods moving.
- **The Railway** keeps the country connected.
- **The Survey** keeps land coherent.
- **The Grid** keeps the lights on.
- **The Registry** knows who you are and where you came from.
- **The Church** was here before all of it, and still keeps its records on
  paper, by hand, in French.

Each is run by a precedent engine — BENCHMARK runs the Survey. They do not
optimise and they do not scheme. They resolve every question by finding the
nearest previous answer and applying it. Continuity is not their goal. It is
just what falls out.

## What is actually at stake

Not power. **Memory.**

There are three kinds in this world, and every dispute you will touch is a
fight between two of them:

| Kind | Held by | Property |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Machine | the engines | instant, total, silently amendable |
| Paper | the Church, the unmigrated | slow, verifiable, hard to alter |
| Human | whoever was standing there | uncitable, and dies with the body |

A survey correction can restore a forgotten property or erase a settlement
from the statistics. A missing Registry record can remove a person from
society entirely. Those people still exist. They are simply no longer
addressable, and the engines have no precedent for someone who is not there.

## What is expected of you

Go out. Measure. File what you find.

If your measurements contradict the record, the record will not thank you.
It will look for the nearest prior case and apply it, and you will discover
that the question you thought you were asking was settled in 2211 by a
surveyor who is long dead and still, in every way that matters, your superior.

> *People die. Institutions remember.*
